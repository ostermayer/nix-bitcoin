Audit complete. HEAD matches the target commit, tree clean. Summary of what was done and found:

## Surface map

The repo is a hardening fork of fort-nix/nix-bitcoin (fork point `37931e5`, 71 fork commits, all reviewed via full diff against the fork point). Shipped surface: NixOS modules for bitcoind, lnd, electrs, nbxplorer/btcpayserver, secrets management (`setup-secrets`, `generate-secrets`), onion-services/onion-addresses, netns-isolation + `netns-exec` (cap_sys_admin wrapper), wireguard preset (single-peer, iptables-guarded lnd REST), operator/doas, backups, plus CI (VM tests, weekly vulnix scan, CalVer release tagging) and a vendored btcpayserver 2.4.4 package with its own 193-entry nuget lockfile.

## Investigation

Every fork-delta file was read in full or as a complete diff: secrets handling, onion hostname copying, lnd macaroon minting (root ExecStartPost), RPC whitelists, netns C wrapper, firewall rules, CI workflows, helper scripts, examples, docs. The fork's hardening changes (symlink-safe onion copy with content validation, deprivileged macaroon reads, atomic staging rename, argv-free rpcauth, netns-exec absolute-path + cap-drop fixes, trimmed public RPC whitelist) are correctly implemented — I traced the threat models they cite and the fixes hold. CI is SHA-pinned with minimal permissions; no `pull_request_target`. The vendored-package hashes and nixpkgs pins are content-addressed but unverifiable from here (future-dated versions). No malicious or careless fork-introduced vulnerability was found; findings below are residual/accepted risks, all but one already self-documented by the fork.
